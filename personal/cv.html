Welcome!

Sophie T Koerner

I'm an early career researcher, a psychologist by training, and passionate about mental health awareness - personally, I would like to see myself as part time academic, part time artist (that means starving, sleep-deprived, running on caffeine and existential dread - but hey, I get to do what I love)

∞ Originally, I intended to create this page to share my non-research projects, past and present. Though I soon realized that contextualization is essential to understanding my creative work ~ for me, art is simply inseparable from the past, the pain and the darkness, for better or for worse. ~∞~ As a survivor of organized child abuse and psychological torture at the hands of several caregivers, I had little choice but to learn to express myselves through whatever means possible - which mostly meant art, dance, photography, writing. While escape was not possible, encryption became vital - the brain compartmentalizes. The human nervous system is a complex structure perfectly equipped to survive hardship and adversity. This is where my fascination with coding and modular design stems from; these allow for the creation of complex systems, easily adaptable to ever-changing needs. ~~∞~~ After years in and out of therapy, numerous suicide attempts, misdiagnoses and dark, shameful, confusing periods in my life... well, the truth about the full extent of past trauma was a bit of a shock, a bit of a relief, and a bit ironic! I, pardon, *we* live with Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID) and complex Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (cptsd). And yet, we live well. Truly. I cherish the calm I/we have found and am grateful to pursue multiple purposes, making this life more than worthwhile. ~~~∞~~~ This space is going to reflect a lot on my healing process, legal proceedings and therapeutic work ...including but not limited to movement-based recovery, reclaiming embodiment and sex therapy (thanks to my sex therapist who has given me one more reason for setting up this website!) Not everyone will be comfortable with that - I understand. I just can't be bothered to feel shame for things that were never mine to own, things done to me - not anymore. I refuse to be shamed for what others have passed onto me and in doing so, I wish to encourage others to do the same. I hope you will find this space a safe and welcoming one, and that you will be able to take something away from it.
